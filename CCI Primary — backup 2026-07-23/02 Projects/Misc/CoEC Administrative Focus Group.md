The literature clearly supports hybrid organizational models that combine centralized infrastructure with distributed, locally responsive services [1]. These models are more effective precisely because they preserve the critical connection between service delivery and the academic units where teaching, research, and student engagement actually occur [2]. Evidence shows that shared services succeed only when governance is clear and strong local alignment is maintained; without this, responsiveness declines and service quality suffers [3]. In this context, well-defined reporting lines and direct alignment with departmental leadership are essential (not optional) as they ensure that staff priorities are guided by those most directly responsible for faculty success, student outcomes, and program quality [4]. Research on higher education reform further demonstrates that structural alignment and active engagement of local leadership are necessary conditions for success, particularly in complex, faculty-driven environments [5]. Embedded professional staff, working in direct partnership with Department Heads and faculty, play a critical role in translating institutional processes into effective, real-time support for students and programs. Sustainable success depends on maintaining direct accountability to departmental leadership, without which service delivery risks becoming disconnected from the student experience and the faculty, students, and programs it is intended to support [6].

### 6. Emerging Needs & Forward-Looking Risks

_16 of 40 respondents (40%) provided emerging needs input — unusually high for a final open-ended question._

#### Enrollment Growth — 🔴 Critical

Advising, instructional, and facilities capacity cannot absorb anticipated growth without proactive planning. Caseload structures and headcount must scale before enrollment increases materialize.

#### Federal Research Funding Volatility — 🔴 Critical

Need to diversify sponsorship beyond federal sources. New business development and grant-writing infrastructure required at the college level.

#### Semester Conversion — 🟡 Monitor

Near-term operational crisis requiring coordinated planning across advising, scheduling, and faculty workload.

#### AI & Lab Infrastructure — 🟢 Notable

Multiple respondents called for AI-assisted advising tools and dedicated lab engineering support as enrollment scales.

### 7. Bottom Line for Leadership

The college is operating at or near capacity across nearly every function. Academic Advising is the most acute pressure point by every measure: highest selection rate, deepest response length, and the strongest resistance to full centralization.

**Highest-ROI interventions:** Shared communication systems, integrated platforms (advising CRM, financial reporting tools), cross-unit training programs, and standardized policies — addressing the top opportunity themes (communication: 70 hits; technology: 61 hits) without displacing embedded relationships.

> **🔴 Immediate action required:** Enrollment growth, federal funding volatility, and semester conversion are present-tense operational risks. Any restructuring model must be stress-tested against these scenarios before implementation.

> **⚠️ Data gap:** CCI respondents engaged only 2.2 areas on average vs. 7.0 for CoE. Dedicated follow-up sessions with CCI leadership are recommended before finalizing college-wide recommendations.
